Iron or dot

What the sight picture can look like with different prescriptions. The eye focuses sharply at one distance at a time: the rear sight is about 55 cm away, the front sight about 70 cm and the target 5–25 m. Pick an example or dial in a prescription yourself.

Eyes and age

Slipping focus
0.3 s

A hypothesis: with one eye minus and one plus, the eyes ask for different focus, and the shared focus hunts between them. Wobble is the natural focus jitter; it grows the harder the eye has to focus, so it hits the front sight far more than the target. Glasses never correct perfectly: “Correction” sets how much of the prescription they fix.

Prescription

Nearsighted (minus)

sphere −

Without glasses the sharp point sits close: at −1.5 around 67 cm, roughly at the front sight. The front sight is sharp almost for free, while the target blurs. At −4 the sharp point is at 25 cm, and everything in the sight picture is blurry.

Farsighted (plus)

sphere +

The eye has to spend focus just to see far. Young eyes manage, but the front sight asks for even more. With age the focus runs out, and the front sight goes first.

Presbyopia

focus range ↓

Focus range shrinks through life. A front sight at 70 cm needs 1.4 dioptres. Around 50 it gets slow, and eventually the front sight blurs while the target stays sharp.

Astigmatism (cylinder)

cylinder × axis

The eye has different power in different directions, so edges blur in one direction: the sides of the front post can be crisp while its top smears. A red dot turns into a line or a comet.

Anisometropia

different R / L

The eyes need different corrections but always focus by the same amount. When the dominant eye has a sharp front sight, the other eye sees something else entirely. With both eyes open the brain usually picks the dominant eye's image.

Red dot

dot ≈ infinity

The dot sits optically far away, in the same focus as the target. One distance, no fight between front sight and target. That's why a dot helps many people with little focus range. Astigmatism still makes the dot star-shaped.

Slipping focus (hypothesis)

one eye −, one eye +

Both eyes share one focus. When one eye is nearsighted and the other farsighted, the front sight needs almost no focus for one eye and a lot for the other. Blur in either eye can pull the focus, so it may hunt: sharp for a moment, then drifting. Glasses help but never correct 100 %: a small difference between the eyes remains, and focus naturally wobbles more the harder it works. So front-sight focus can stay unstable while target focus, which needs almost no effort, holds. Ask an optician or orthoptist; this page only illustrates the idea.

A small pupil helps

pupil 2–7 mm

Blur grows with the pupil. In bright light the pupil shrinks and more distances look sharp at once. That's why precision shooters may use an iris aperture in front of the eye.

Simplified model. Blur is the distance in dioptres between where the eye focuses and each object, times the pupil size. It ignores image size, aberrations and the brain suppressing one eye. The astigmatism direction is approximate, and ages are averages; people vary a lot.

Not a vision test. If you wonder about your own sight picture, bring the pistol (or the distances) to an optician.
